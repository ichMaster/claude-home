# t1144 — astro v2, phase 2: explain his facts ourselves, and be graded on it

> Outline. Phase 2 of `docs/astro-v2-reference.md`. The test cases below are the ones already
> certain; the pass mark is deliberately not invented here — it is set by the owner after the
> first honest measurement exists, because a target named before the first number is a guess
> dressed as a gate.

## Problem / goal

Phase 1 translates his meanings. Phase 2 stops using them: the facts of a day go in with his
interpretations stripped off, our own interpretation comes out, and his text for that same day
is the answer sheet. The point is not a score — it is the **vocabulary** that falls out of the
comparison: factor → meaning, each entry traceable to the day it was learned from.

The route from factor to meaning is the thing v1 failed at three times with correct figures,
so this is the phase that decides whether v2 is worth having.

## What a run does

1. **Strip** — his day, as structured by phase 1's reader, with every `meaning` field removed:
   the Moon's sign, house and degree, the void window, the ingress, each timed aspect with its
   formula and intensity, each personal transit. Same fact set as his, so only the reading
   differs.
2. **Interpret** (`status/mood2/explain.md`) — our meaning per factor, with the accumulated
   vocabulary and the `ПРАВИЛО:` lines from `status/state/astro-reference-rules.md` in the
   prompt. One factor, one meaning, no hedging across both directions.
3. **Grade** (`status/mood2/grade.md`) — our meaning against his, per factor:
   `same` / `close` / `opposite` / `missing`, with the reason. Stored to
   `status/state/astro2/agreement-<day>.json`.
4. **Distil** — every `opposite` and every `missing` becomes a vocabulary entry or a
   `ПРАВИЛО:` line, append-only, sourced by day. An `opposite` is the valuable one: it is a
   textbook reading that this astrologer rejects, like his square to Venus meaning indulgence
   rather than friction.

## Test cases

- **TC1 —** the facts handed to the interpreter carry no trace of his meanings: no `meaning`
  field, no sentence of his text, in the prompt or the fact lines.
- **TC2 —** grading a day produces one verdict per factor of his text, with no factor
  silently dropped; the stored file totals match the factor count.
- **TC3 —** an `opposite` verdict appends exactly one vocabulary entry or rule, with the day
  it came from; the store is append-only (a line is never edited or removed).
- **TC4 —** scoring is computed over days the interpreter was not shown his text for; a day
  used as a reference is excluded from its own score.
- **TC5 —** with fewer than `astro2.min_graded_days` days on file, the score reports
  "not enough data" rather than a percentage — the same honesty rule the claim score already
  follows.
- **TC6 — edge:** a factor he names that our fact set does not contain is graded `missing`
  and recorded as a gap for phase 3, not quietly ignored.

## Approach

Deterministic where it can be: the stripping, the bookkeeping, the score. Model calls only for
the interpretation and the grading, and the grader never sees which side is ours.

Gated by data volume, not by code: roughly two weeks of his calendar before a number means
anything, a month before a rule learned from it is worth trusting. See the data position in
the concept document.

Deliberately out of scope: computing the factors ourselves (t1145), and changing phase 1's
delivery.
