# t1215 — parse_due turns version and decimal numbers into deadlines

## Problem / goal

The dotted-pair branch of `parse_due` accepts any `d.m` with day ≤ 31 and month ≤ 12:
`parse_due("оновити python 2.7")` returns due 2027-07-02 with the title mutilated to
«оновити python», and «бюджет 10.5 тис» becomes due 2027-05-10, title «бюджет тис». Via
`/task` in Telegram this lands silently: the task shows a bogus next-year deadline the
probe and digest then report. The t1048 guard only caught impossible dates (31.11, 3.14),
and its own comment names «python 3.14» as a number-not-date — version/decimal tokens
becoming deadlines contradicts the parser's stated intent. Found by the nightly
self-review of 2026-11-19 (Medium).

## Test cases

- **TC1 —** «оновити python 2.7» parses with no due date and the title intact.
  (regression — fails before the change)
- **TC2 —** «бюджет 10.5 тис» parses with no due date and the title intact. (regression)
- **TC3 — edge:** real dates keep working: «звіт до 30.11», «зустріч 18.10 в кафе»,
  «до 15.01» with the next-year rollover (existing t1048/t1088/t1184 tests re-run green).

## Approach

Two skip rules on a dotted-pair candidate, both cheap and documented in place:
a pair immediately preceded by a Latin-script word is a version («python 2.7», «v1.2»,
«node 18.10»), and a pair immediately followed by a measure word or sign
(тис/млн/млрд/грн/кг/км/мм/см/%) is a decimal. Deliberately out of scope: a Latin word
before a genuine date («deadline 20.12») now reads as no-date — tasks here are written
in Ukrainian, and a silent wrong deadline costs more than an unparsed one (the t1048
principle).
